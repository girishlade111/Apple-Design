We're carbon neutral.
And we won't stop there.

Every product, every process, every step of the way.

Learn more about our plan

We've been working toward carbon neutrality for over a decade. In 2020, we set a goal to make our entire carbon footprint net zero by 2030 — and we achieved it. Now we're going further.

From the materials we source to the energy that powers every Apple facility, from the way we design our packaging to how our products are recycled — every decision is an opportunity to protect the planet we all share. This isn't just a corporate initiative. It's our responsibility.

Clean energy, everywhere.

Every Apple facility — every office, every retail store, every data center — is powered by 100% renewable energy. And we're bringing our suppliers along.

More than 300 suppliers in over 30 countries have committed to using 100% renewable energy for Apple production. Together, we're building one of the largest renewable energy programs in the world, generating over 16 gigawatts of clean power commitments.

Our investments in solar, wind, and other renewable energy sources don't just reduce our footprint — they help accelerate the global transition to clean energy, creating jobs and economic opportunity in communities around the world.

Recycled. Redesigned. Reimagined.

We're increasing our use of recycled and renewable materials across all our products. Here's what that looks like.

The latest MacBook Air uses 100% recycled aluminum in its enclosure. iPhone 17 Pro uses 100% recycled cobalt in its battery, 100% recycled tin in the solder of its main logic board, and 100% recycled rare earth elements in all magnets.

We've also pioneered innovations like Daisy, our disassembly robot that can take apart 1.2 million iPhones a year, recovering materials that traditional recyclers can't. Every component recovered is another step toward a closed supply chain.

Less packaging. More thinking.

We eliminated plastic from nearly all our packaging. But we didn't stop at substitution — we redesigned from the ground up.

By making packaging smaller and lighter, we can fit more boxes on each shipping pallet. That means fewer trucks, fewer ships, and a smaller carbon footprint from transportation. The iPhone 17 Pro box alone ships in a package that's 95% fiber-based, using responsibly sourced paper and recycled content.
